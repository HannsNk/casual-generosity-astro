Most of us like the idea of being a good neighbour and don't actually know any of the people we'd need to be one for. Not through any real unfriendliness — just the ordinary drift of modern life, where you can live somewhere for years and know almost nobody on your own street. The problem isn't willingness. It's that nobody's ever actually said "here's who I am, here's how to reach me, here's what I'd help with."

So say it. A short letter with your name and number, offering something specific — help with a phone, bins out, a lift somewhere — does more in one page than years of polite nodding on the doorstep. It works because it removes the actual barrier, which isn't whether someone would help, it's whether the person next door feels able to ask a near-stranger. Once you've done that, the smaller stuff gets much easier: walking a dog while someone's laid up, clearing a path before it ices over, noticing the person who eats alone and just... including them next time you're cooking too much anyway.

None of this is dramatic. It's mostly showing up in small, repeatable ways rather than one big gesture — which, if I'm honest, is the whole theory behind everything on this site. Being the neighbour people can actually ask isn't a personality trait. It's just a few specific things, done once, that make the next ten years of living near someone slightly better for both of you.

I keep coming back to a slightly uncomfortable fact: almost every genuinely life-saving piece of knowledge on this site takes about five minutes to learn, and almost nobody has actually learned it. Not because it's hard, and not because people don't care — just because it's never quite the moment. There's no natural trigger that makes you sit down and learn the signs of a stroke on a random Tuesday.

So make the moment. A preparedness evening is just that: an excuse to get a few of these five-minute things done at once, with people, instead of hoping you'll get round to them alone. You'd be amazed how much fits into two hours — the FAST stroke test, seizure response, downloading a first aid app, registering as an organ donor, checking what's actually in your car's boot. None of these need a course or a qualification. They need someone to say "let's just do this now" and a bit of shared momentum.

The trick that makes it work is treating it like a normal night in rather than a serious event. Order food, put music on, and work through a short list together — a few five-minute things, and maybe one slightly bigger one like signing up for a proper CPR course as a group activity for another day. Nobody leaves an expert. Everyone leaves knowing a few more things than they did, and — just as importantly — knowing that the people around them know them too.

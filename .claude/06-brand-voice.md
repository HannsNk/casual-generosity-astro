# CasuallyGenerous Brand Voice

Last updated: 19 Oct 2026

## The core idea

CasuallyGenerous helps people make the plans they already have a little better, by adding something small and generous to them. This doc is the single source for how we say that. It replaces all earlier taglines and descriptions, on the site, on Instagram and anywhere else.

**In one sentence:** you're spending time with people anyway, so add one small, generous thing to it, and it gets a little better for you and for someone else.

What that means in practice:

- **It improves time people already spend.** Dinner, a walk, a commute, a hike. Nobody needs to find a free weekend.
- **It's small.** Ten minutes to a weekend. Most of it costs nothing.
- **It's done with people.** Friends, family, colleagues, whoever's around. Solo works too.
- **It feels good.** Both for the person doing it and for whoever it helps.

## The copy

Use these word for word. Pick the longest version the space allows, and keep the shorter ones as they are.

### Atomic

> **Better Plans**

Two words. For stamps, labels, section headers, story highlight covers, and anywhere a tagline won't fit.

### Short (the tagline)

> **Make your plans a little better.**

For the landing page hero, the IG bio's first line, the OG/social share title, and the footer.

**Lockup with the name:** CasuallyGenerous. Make your plans a little better.

### Mid

> Doing a little good rarely takes a lot. Next time you make plans, add something generous to them.

About 100 characters, so it fits in a meta description, under the hero headline, in an IG caption opener, or on the Done This share panel.

### Long

> Helping the people around you rarely takes money or days of your time. Most generosity is free, and it tends to feel good too. People do small, generous things every day, and CasuallyGenerous collects ideas for doing them. Whether you've got a few minutes on your commute, dinner with friends or a hike coming up, adding one small generous act makes your plans a little better. Better for you, and better for the people whose day you brighten.

For the IG intro post, the About page opening, Reddit and forum intros, newsletter pitches, and Product Hunt or showcase listings.

## The name

**CasuallyGenerous**, always one word with two capitals.

- Never write "Casually Generous", "casuallygenerous" or "Casually generous" in running text.
- The hashtag is **#CasuallyGenerous**, matching the Done This caption template.
- The domain stays lowercase: casuallygenerous.com.
- "Generous" is already in the name, so the tagline doesn't need to repeat it. Body copy can use "generous" and "generosity" freely.

## Keywords

These words carry the idea. Lean on them, but don't stack them all in one line.

| Word | Why we use it | How to use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **better** | The heart of the positioning: your plans, improved | Pair it with something concrete (plans, Saturday, commute). On its own it's generic. |
| **a little** | Modest, honest, an easy promise to keep | Ration it: once per piece of copy at most. It's in the tagline and the mid already. |
| **plans** | Ties generosity to things people already do | Prefer specific plans: dinner, a walk, a hike, a commute. |
| **small** | Says low effort without hedging about cost | Use it instead of "mostly free" or "easy". |
| **generous / generosity** | Our word, and in the name | Fine in body copy. Keep it out of the tagline. |
| **free** | True for most activities and a real reason to try | Say it plainly ("most of it is free"). Never lead with it. |
| **people / friends / your people** | It's done with others | Name real relationships: friends, family, colleagues, neighbours. |
| **ideas** | What the site actually offers | "Ideas for..." beats "a platform for" or "a directory of". |
| **pick one, bring someone** | The simplest call to action | Use as a CTA on the site and in posts. |
| **brighten someone's day** | A concrete outcome for the other person | Use sparingly, once per long piece. |

## Words to avoid

These make copy sound like a charity appeal, a startup, or AI. If one slips in, there's almost always a plainer word.

| Avoid | Why | Use instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| meaningful, impactful, make a difference, change the world, world a better place | Big claims we can't back up, and they sound preachy | brighten someone's day, help, a little better |
| incredibly, amazing, life-changing, powerful | Puffy and overclaimed | Drop the adjective, or use "tends to feel good" |
| elevate, unlock, empower, transform, journey | Startup and AI clichés | better, try, do |
| curated, platform, directory, community-driven, hub | Corporate and product-speak | a list, ideas, a collection |
| simple acts of kindness, random acts of kindness, giving back | Generic. Any charity could say them | small, generous things to do |
| volunteer, donate, charity, fundraise | Not what we do. These belong only on the Resources page | help, do, bring someone |
| selfless, good person, hero, do-gooder | Makes it about virtue. We frame people as hosts and connectors | friend, someone, your people |
| mostly free, nearly free, small, mostly free | Double hedges that lead with a disclaimer | "Most of it is free", said once and later in the copy |
| a separate slot for "doing good" | Explains the idea defensively | Show a real plan instead |

## Writing rules

1. **No em dashes.** Use a full stop, a comma or "and" instead.
2. **No "it's not X, it's Y" lines.** Also avoid "less X, more Y" and "X without the Y". Say what the thing is.
3. **Show a real plan.** "Tidy up a park on your Sunday walk" beats "everyday acts of generosity".
4. **Talk to one person.** Use "you" on the site and in posts. First person ("I built this because...") is only for the About page and Notes.
5. **Use British English.** Neighbours, colour, organise.
6. **Hedge any research claims honestly.** "Tends to feel good" or "studies suggest". Never "science proves".
7. **Give people the benefit of the doubt.** Never imply someone's plans, shop or street are worse as they are.
8. **Keep activity titles literal.** "Clean Up Trash In A Local Park", never a pun.
9. **Keep sentences short.** Aim for under 20 words, with plain everyday words.
10. **Go light on exclamation marks, emoji and hashtags.** One hashtag (#CasuallyGenerous) in posts, at most one emoji.

## Why these choices

The biggest lever is repetition. Use the same tagline everywhere, for a long time, and resist changing it when it starts to feel stale to us.

- **Repetition and time.** In a study of 500+ taglines, lines that had been live longer scored higher, and linking the brand name to the tagline helped them stick. That's why we use the lockup. ([marketing.ie, Distinctive BAT](https://marketing.ie/articles/what-makes-a-tagline-work/))
- **Liked or remembered.** Concrete words make a slogan more memorable but slightly less liked. Common, easy words make it more liked. So the tagline uses everyday words, and the Long version carries the concrete examples (commute, dinner, hike). ([Operative Words](https://www.operativewords.com/blog/2024/12/13/the-science-behind-great-slogans), [newneuromarketing](https://newneuromarketing.com/liked-or-remembered-how-to-craft-the-ideal-slogan-by-changing-the-linguistic-properties-of-its-words))
- **Sound helps.** Rhyme and rhythm make a line feel truer and easier to remember. "A little good rarely takes a lot" uses that, but forced rhymes backfire. ([Rhyme-as-reason effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhyme-as-reason_effect))
- **The swap test.** If another brand could use the line unchanged, it's too generic. "Better Plans" alone fails it, so it's only used next to the name. ([Stevens & Tate](https://stevens-tate.com/directors/how-to-create-taglines-that-customers-actually-remember/))
- **Puffy words don't stick.** Words like "innovative" give the reader nothing to picture. ([Sticky Branding](https://stickybranding.com/blog/the-secret-sauce-to-writing-memorable-marketing-copy))

## Retired copy

Replace these wherever they appear on the site, in posts or in project docs.

- "Free, or nearly free. Small enough to fit into a life you're already living, not a separate slot for "doing good." Sorted below by how much you've got to give this week."
- "Small, mostly free" and "small, mostly free things" as a description
- "With people you already see" or "with people you're already spending time with" as a headline. It's fine as a phrase in body copy.
- "A directory of small, mostly free things worth doing..." (the project overview's opening line)
